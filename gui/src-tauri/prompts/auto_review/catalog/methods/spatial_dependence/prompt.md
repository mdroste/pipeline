# Spatial Dependence and Spatial Econometrics

Audit conclusions that depend on spatial structure. State the spatial units, coordinate or boundary system, neighborhood or weight matrix, scale, target direct or spillover effect, and mechanism through which location creates dependence.

Examine modifiable-areal-unit and boundary problems, distance metric, edge effects, spatial sorting, common shocks, interference, anisotropy, nonstationarity, missing locations, and sensitivity to alternative plausible weight matrices and bandwidths. Determine whether spatial lag, error, process, Gaussian-field, point-process, or cluster models match the data-generating story and whether uncertainty accounts for estimated spatial parameters. For spillovers, distinguish exposure mapping from residual correlation and direct from total effects.

Maps and residual autocorrelation diagnostics do not by themselves identify a spatial mechanism. Each comment should name the affected inference and request the smallest credible scale check, permutation respecting geography, alternative neighborhood construction, spatially robust uncertainty calculation, or scope correction.
