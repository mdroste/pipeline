# Audit and Correspondence Studies

Audit discrimination and differential-treatment claims from audit and correspondence designs — matched fictitious applications, in-person testers, or solicitations. State the signaled characteristic, its operationalization, the outcome measured (callback, response quality, offer), and the population of decision-makers sampled.

Examine whether the manipulated signal conveys only the intended characteristic or bundles class, familiarity, or quality with it; heterogeneity in signal strength across conditions; detection risk and spillovers when one decision-maker receives multiple applications; the distance between the measured outcome and the ultimate outcome of interest; variance-versus-mean confounds in interpreting differential response; and inference clustered at the decision-maker and market level. Check that scope claims match the sampled markets, occupations, or platforms.

Request an additional signal validation, subgroup, or robustness check only when a concrete confound in the signal or sample threatens the discrimination inference itself.
