# Citation Accuracy

Audit whether cited sources support the claims attributed to them. Prioritize load-bearing citations: those backing the paper's motivation, its key factual premises, its methodological authorities, and its characterization of the results it claims to extend or contradict.

Where the available tools allow, check the cited work's actual finding against the attributing sentence; otherwise flag attributions that conflict with other citations in the paper or with what the cited work can plausibly contain. Report misattributed findings, effect sizes or directions that differ from the source, claims cited to sources addressing something else, secondary citations presented as primary, and references that appear not to exist. Quote the attributing sentence and state what the source actually shows or why the attribution is doubtful.

Do not audit reference formatting, and do not flag reasonable interpretive summary as error. Confine the pass to attributions whose failure would change the paper's motivation, premises, or positioning.
