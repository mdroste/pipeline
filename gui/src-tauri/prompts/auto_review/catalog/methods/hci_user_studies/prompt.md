# HCI and Usability Studies

Audit claims from human-computer interaction and usability evaluations — lab studies, field deployments, or comparative interface experiments. State the tasks, the participants and their recruitment, the design (within or between subjects), the measures, and the claims about the system or technique.

Examine novelty and demand effects when researchers evaluate their own system; task realism and coverage relative to the claimed use; ordering and learning controls in within-subject designs; baseline implementation quality and tuning parity; the validity of task time, error, and questionnaire measures for the experience constructs claimed; the participant population against the intended users, including any accessibility claims; and attrition and self-selection in deployments. Check that the statistical treatment matches the design's clustering and repeated measures.

Request an additional baseline, counterbalancing check, or scoped claim only when a concrete design artifact threatens the comparative or usability conclusion.
