# Interviews and Focus Groups

Audit claims supported by interview testimony or group discussion. State who was eligible, who was recruited and declined, interviewer-participant relationships, setting and mode, question structure, recording and transcription, language and translation, and analytic use of responses.

Examine sampling relative to the claimed perspectives; elite access and organizational selection; recall, social-desirability, interviewer, and status effects; leading prompts; focus-group conformity and confidentiality; saturation claims; coding and adjudication; contradictory testimony; and triangulation with behavior, documents, or other sources. Distinguish what participants experienced, believed, justified, or retrospectively narrated from independent evidence that an event or mechanism occurred. Quotations should support rather than substitute for the analytic pattern.

Each issue should identify the testimony and inference affected and propose a sampling clarification, interview-guide disclosure, negative-case analysis, coding audit, source triangulation, translation check, or narrower claim about whose account is represented.
