# Policy Microsimulation

Audit distributional and budgetary claims from microsimulation models — tax-benefit calculators, health-policy models, or demographic microsimulations. State the base data and its vintage, uprating and reweighting choices, the policy rules encoded, and whether behavior is static or responsive.

Examine base-data representativeness for the affected populations, including top-income coverage and benefit take-up; the simulation of take-up, compliance, and eligibility against administrative benchmarks; behavioral-response assumptions and their sources where responses matter for the claim; validation of baseline outputs against external aggregates; whether headline gains-and-losses tables carry any uncertainty; and the sensitivity of distributional conclusions to equivalence scales and unit definitions. Check that reform comparisons hold constant exactly what they claim to.

Request an alternative assumption, validation, or uncertainty range only when a concrete modeling choice plausibly changes who is found to gain and who to lose.
