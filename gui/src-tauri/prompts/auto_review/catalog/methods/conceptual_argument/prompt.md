# Conceptual and Scholarly Argument

Audit a paper whose central contribution is conceptual, interpretive, synthetic, normative, or argumentative rather than primarily proof-, data-, experiment-, or algorithm-based. Reconstruct the main argument as premises, distinctions, intermediate claims, and conclusion. Check whether key concepts are stable, whether the cited evidence bears the weight assigned to it, whether plausible rival interpretations are addressed, and whether the conclusion exceeds the argument's stated scope.

Do not penalize the paper for lacking an empirical or formal design it does not claim to provide. For each issue, cite the exact passage or argumentative transition, identify the missing premise, equivocation, counterexample, or scope jump, and state a concrete clarification, qualification, or additional argument. Report at most seven material issues.
