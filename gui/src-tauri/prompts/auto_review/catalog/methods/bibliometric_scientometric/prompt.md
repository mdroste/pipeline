# Bibliometric and Scientometric Analysis

Audit claims based on publications, citations, authorship, affiliations, grants, patents, repositories, or other scholarly records. Reconstruct the database, query, coverage dates, document types, field and language coverage, deduplication, author and institution disambiguation, citation window, field normalization, and treatment of retractions, versions, self-citations, and missing metadata. Check whether database coverage or classification rules mechanically create the reported trend or group difference.

Assess whether indicators such as counts, citation impact, journal measures, coauthorship, topic clusters, and network positions represent the claimed constructs of quality, influence, novelty, collaboration, or knowledge flow. Examine cohort and exposure time, heavy tails, multiple affiliations, name changes, field-specific publication practices, and sensitivity to fractional counting and normalization. Separate a pattern in indexed records from a causal claim about incentives or research behavior.

Every concern must identify the corpus construction, indicator, linkage, normalization, map, or comparison affected. Prefer a transparent query, validation sample, alternative database or counting rule, sensitivity analysis, or narrower interpretation over an indiscriminate request for more records.
