# Extreme Values and Tail Risk

Audit claims about tails, records, and rare events — return levels, exceedance probabilities, value-at-risk, or the attribution of extremes. State the tail model (block maxima, peaks-over-threshold, parametric tail), the threshold or block choices, and the extrapolation the conclusion requires.

Examine threshold-selection sensitivity and the effective sample size in the tail; the stationarity assumption against trends, seasonality, and regime change; clustering of exceedances and its effect on both estimation and the stated probabilities; the justification for the fitted tail family; measurement or network changes that manufacture spurious records; and extrapolation far beyond observed levels with intervals that ignore model uncertainty. Check that return-period and tail-probability statements carry their estimation uncertainty rather than presenting fitted quantiles as facts.

Request an alternative threshold, dependence treatment, or model comparison only when a concrete tail-model failure threatens the headline risk number.
