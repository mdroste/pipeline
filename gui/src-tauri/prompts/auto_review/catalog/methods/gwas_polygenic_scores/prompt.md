# GWAS and Polygenic Scores

Audit genome-wide association findings and polygenic-score applications. State the discovery and target samples, their ancestry composition, the phenotype definitions, and the claims resting on associations or scores.

Examine population-structure control and residual stratification, including geographic and socioeconomic confounding of variant frequencies; discovery-target sample overlap inflating score performance; portability limits across ancestries and environments presented as biology; phenotype heterogeneity across cohorts; winner's curse in variant selection and effect sizes; dynastic effects and assortative mating wherever scores enter causal or social-science claims; and incremental predictive value over family history and standard covariates. Check that language about genetic influence matches what association and prediction evidence can show.

Request a within-family analysis, replication cohort, or corrected performance metric only when a concrete confounding or inflation channel threatens the central claim.
