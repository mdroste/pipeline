# Bayesian Inference

Audit the Bayesian model as a joint set of substantive assumptions, prior information, likelihood, and computation. State the target parameters or predictions, hierarchy, prior structure, data model, and posterior quantity used for each central claim.

Examine prior support and scale, prior-data conflict, shrinkage and pooling, identifiability, parameterization, likelihood misspecification, exchangeability, model comparison, and sensitivity of decisions or scientific conclusions to defensible alternative priors. Check posterior computation through convergence, effective sample size, chain mixing, divergences, multimodality, approximation error, and simulation-based or posterior-predictive diagnostics. Distinguish credible intervals from frequentist guarantees and Bayes factors from automatic evidence measures; inspect dependence on prior odds and model space.

Focus comments on posterior conclusions that could materially change. Request a prior-predictive check, sensitivity analysis, reparameterization, calibrated computation, model expansion, or narrower interpretation rather than objecting generically to subjective priors.
