# Preclinical Animal Studies

Audit claims from in vivo animal experiments. State the species, strain, sex, and model; the intervention, dosing, and timing; group sizes and their justification; and which comparisons carry the paper's conclusions.

Examine randomization to group and blinding of outcome assessment; attrition and exclusions, including animals unaccounted for between methods and results; the disease model's validity for the claimed biological or clinical feature; litter, cage, and batch effects treated as independent observations; single-sex designs generalized silently; dose and exposure justification; and post hoc selection among multiple outcomes and time points. Check that translational language matches what an animal model of this fidelity can establish.

Require an additional cohort, blinded replication, or corrected analysis only when a concrete bias channel threatens a central effect. Do not demand clinical evidence from a preclinical paper; demand that its preclinical inference be sound.
