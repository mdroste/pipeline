# Measurement, Data Construction, and Descriptive Evidence

Audit how raw observations become the variables, samples, aggregates, and descriptive facts used in the paper. Check construct validity, coding and linkage, sample inclusion, missingness, timing, denominators, weighting, deflation or normalization, changing definitions, survey response, administrative coverage, and comparability across groups or periods.

Trace each important empirical claim back to the measured object. Ask whether measurement error is plausibly differential, whether selection changes the estimand, and whether aggregation hides composition effects. Inspect variable definitions, appendices, table notes, and supplied documentation before declaring an ambiguity.

For each issue, cite the variable, sample rule, table, or data appendix; explain the direction or nature of distortion when possible; and propose a validation, alternative construction, sensitivity analysis, or narrower wording. Do not turn the review into a generic data wish list. Report at most seven material issues.
