# Sufficient Statistics and Welfare Analysis

Audit welfare and optimal-policy claims derived from sufficient-statistics formulas — envelope-based deadweight-loss, optimal-tax, or social-insurance calculations that plug reduced-form elasticities into theoretical formulas. State the formula, its derivation assumptions, and each empirical input with its source.

Examine whether the behavioral responses used are the ones the formula requires — compensated versus uncompensated, short-run versus long-run, micro versus macro elasticities; envelope conditions broken by fixed costs, frictions, misoptimization, or externalities; fiscal-externality terms omitted from the calculation; the match between the population in which inputs were estimated and the population the welfare claim covers; and the propagation of input uncertainty into the welfare number. Check that optimality claims state the planner's objective and constraint set rather than leaving them implicit.

Request an additional input, correction term, or sensitivity range only when a concrete assumption failure would change the sign or order of magnitude of the welfare conclusion.
