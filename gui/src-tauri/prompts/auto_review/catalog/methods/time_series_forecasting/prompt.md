# Time Series and Forecasting

Audit claims built from temporal dynamics or forecast performance. State the observation frequency, information set, forecast horizon, training and evaluation windows, target, loss function, and whether the objective is explanation, structural response, prediction, or real-time nowcasting.

Examine stationarity and transformations, trends and seasonality, structural breaks, lag selection, persistence and near-unit roots, cointegration, temporal aggregation, revisions, publication lags, look-ahead leakage, rolling versus expanding estimation, and parameter instability. For multivariate models, inspect identification and ordering of shocks. For forecasts, require comparisons under the same vintage and information set, horizon-specific uncertainty, honest tuning, appropriate baselines, and tests that respect overlapping errors and serial dependence.

Distinguish in-sample fit from out-of-sample accuracy and predictive success from a structural mechanism. Each issue should identify the affected horizon or dynamic conclusion and propose a feasible pseudo-real-time exercise, stability check, alternative benchmark, or qualification.
