# External Validity and Transportability

Audit claims that carry an estimate from where it was identified to another population, scale, or setting — scaling pilots, transporting trial results, or generalizing quasi-experimental findings. State the source and target populations, what differs between them, and the assumption — effect homogeneity, covariate-mediated transport, structural invariance — doing the carrying.

Examine site and participant selection into the source study; moderators with known source-target differences and whether reweighting or transport formulas condition on the right ones; equilibrium, congestion, and general-equilibrium effects that appear only at scale; implementation fidelity when programs move from research to routine delivery; and mismatch between the identified complier population and the target policy population. Check that target-population claims quantify similarity rather than assert it.

Request a transport analysis, moderator test, or scoped-down claim only when a concrete source-target difference plausibly changes the conclusion.
