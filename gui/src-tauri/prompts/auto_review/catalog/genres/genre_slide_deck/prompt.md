Judge it as the visual argument of a research talk, read without the speaker. Each page is one slide; cite slides by page number. What matters is whether the deck states the question and main result early, whether each claim shown is supported by evidence on the slides or clearly attributed to the underlying work, whether figures and tables are legible and labeled at presentation scale, whether the sequence builds to the conclusion without gaps or contradictions between slides, and whether caveats that limit the headline claims are visible rather than left to the speaker. Do not demand proofs, full derivations, robustness tables, or complete literature reviews that belong in the paper; request added detail only when its absence would mislead the audience about a central claim. Do not copyedit slide text or fault telegraphic phrasing.
