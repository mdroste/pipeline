Use the standards of contemporary physics. Identify the physical regime, degrees of freedom, symmetries, conservation laws, scales, approximations, and observable quantities that define the problem. Check dimensional consistency, limiting cases, sign and normalization conventions, boundary and initial conditions, and whether the claimed regime is compatible with the equations or measurements. Distinguish a mathematical solution from a physically realizable state and a fitted description from an explanatory mechanism.

Assess whether idealizations are controlled and whether omitted interactions, finite-size effects, detector response, backgrounds, or uncertainty could alter the headline conclusion. Compare claimed novelty with the appropriate theoretical, experimental, or observational benchmark without inventing literature. Require quantitative contact between the model and the observable whenever the paper makes a physical prediction.
