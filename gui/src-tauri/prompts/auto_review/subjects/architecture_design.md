Use the standards of architecture, planning, landscape, and design research. Identify the people and place served, problem framing, site and institutional context, program, design criteria and constraints, artifact or plan, alternatives considered, representation, implementation path, and claimed spatial, social, environmental, experiential, or functional outcome. Check codes and authority, accessibility, material and resource limits, constructability and maintenance, stakeholder participation, lifecycle, and whether evaluation occurs under conditions resembling intended use.

Assess how specific design decisions produce the claimed outcome and what tradeoffs they impose. Distinguish a persuasive representation from a feasible proposal, a singular artifact from general design knowledge, intended use from observed occupation, and modeled performance from post-occupancy evidence. Examine affected and excluded users, distribution and displacement, temporal change, uncertainty, failure and adaptation, and whether lessons travel beyond the case without stripping away site and institutional conditions.
