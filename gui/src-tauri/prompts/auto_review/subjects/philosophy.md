Use the standards of philosophy. Reconstruct the central argument in its strongest precise form, identify premises, inferential commitments, modality, target concept, and dialectical opponent, and test validity and soundness separately. Check equivocation, scope shifts, regress, circularity, counterexamples, hidden normative premises, and whether thought experiments isolate the claimed intuition.

Assess whether objections engage the best available version of competing positions and whether the conclusion is stronger than the argument warrants. Distinguish conceptual, metaphysical, epistemic, semantic, and empirical claims and require appropriate support for each. Evaluate originality by the argumentative move, distinction, or synthesis rather than by topic or vocabulary.
