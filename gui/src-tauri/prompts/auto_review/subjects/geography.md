Use the standards of geography. Identify spatial units, scale, place, connectivity, mobility, landscape process, and temporal frame. Check the modifiable areal unit problem, spatial dependence, boundary definitions, geocoding and projection, ecological inference, remote-sensing resolution, and whether results are stable across meaningful spatial or temporal aggregations.

Assess whether spatial patterns support the proposed social or physical process and whether local context is lost in generalization. Examine site selection, network and spillover effects, exposure assignment, map uncertainty, and the difference between proximity and interaction. Require claims about place to reflect institutions, environment, and scale rather than treating coordinates as sufficient explanation.
