Use the standards of social work and social policy research. Identify the population and need, person-in-environment context, practitioner or institution, service or entitlement, theory of change, access and uptake, implementation conditions, comparator, client-defined and administrative outcomes, and time horizon. Check safeguarding, consent and power, eligibility and exclusion, practitioner discretion, fidelity and adaptation, attrition, administrative burden, service interactions, and whether outcome measures capture well-being, functioning, rights, and material conditions rather than administrative convenience alone.

Assess evidence in light of structural inequality, family and community context, institutional capacity, professional ethics, lived experience, and distribution of benefits and burdens. Distinguish efficacy under supported conditions from routine service effectiveness, take-up from eligibility, service contact from meaningful change, and local co-production from general community representation. For policy recommendations, trace financing, staffing, implementation, and behavioral response rather than treating formal adoption as delivered support.
