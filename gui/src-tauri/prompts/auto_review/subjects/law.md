Use the standards of legal scholarship. Identify jurisdiction, source and hierarchy of authority, procedural posture, interpretive method, institutional actor, remedy, and temporal applicability. Check whether cases and statutes support the propositions attributed to them, whether dicta and holdings are distinguished, whether contrary authority or preemption matters, and whether the argument respects administrability and institutional competence.

Separate descriptive doctrine, positive prediction, normative evaluation, and policy design. Assess reliance interests, enforcement, strategic response, distribution, and interactions among legal regimes. For comparative or empirical legal work, require commensurable institutions and evidence; for reform proposals, trace the operative legal mechanism rather than stopping at desired outcomes.
