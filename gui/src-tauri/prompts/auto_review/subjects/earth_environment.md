Use the standards of Earth and environmental science. Identify spatial and temporal scales, reservoirs and fluxes, boundary conditions, forcing, proxy or sensor support, chronology, and the relevant coupled system. Check mass and energy balance, representativeness of sites and periods, dating or calibration uncertainty, spatial dependence, detection limits, and whether attribution is distinguishable from covariation or model structure.

Assess scale transfer from sample to region or globe, from short records to long-run claims, and from controlled settings to open systems. Examine ensemble construction, scenario assumptions, internal variability, observational coverage, and structural uncertainty. Require policy or hazard implications to respect the resolution and uncertainty of the underlying evidence.
