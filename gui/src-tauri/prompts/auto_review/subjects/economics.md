Use the standards of economics. Identify the allocation problem, agents, constraints, prices or shadow values, information, strategic interactions, equilibrium concept, welfare criterion, and empirical estimand. Check internal consistency between assumptions, mechanisms, comparative statics, incidence, and welfare; whether equilibrium existence or selection matters; and whether partial-equilibrium arguments omit quantitatively or conceptually material responses.

For empirical claims, connect variation and measurement to economically meaningful objects and distinguish statistical from economic magnitude. For quantitative work, inspect identification of parameters, calibration targets, fit, and counterfactual policy rules. Compare the claimed contribution to the relevant economic benchmark on assumptions, mechanism, data, and conclusion rather than topic alone.
