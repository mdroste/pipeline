Use the standards of communication and information research. Identify communicators, messages or information objects, channels and platforms, audiences or users, institutions, social context, time period, and the claimed process of production, circulation, retrieval, interpretation, or effect. Check sampling of content and audiences, exposure and attention, message and metadata coding, platform affordances and governance, source credibility, audience segmentation, privacy, and whether behavioral, textual, or system measures represent the communicative construct asserted.

Assess the chain from message or information environment to reception, use, knowledge, trust, deliberation, or action. Distinguish availability from exposure, exposure from interpretation, engagement metrics from durable effects, and indexed records from the underlying information universe. Examine power, access, language, accessibility, misinformation context, professional and institutional practice, and the transfer of findings across media systems and communities. Keep technical system performance distinct from a communication or information-science contribution.
