Use the standards of historical scholarship. Identify period, place, actors, chronology, source base, historiographical intervention, and the scale of explanation. Check provenance, authorship, audience, genre, preservation, translation, representativeness, and silences in the archive. Distinguish what contemporaneous sources establish from retrospective interpretation and what the evidence dates precisely from what is only broadly sequenced.

Assess whether causal or comparative claims respect chronology and contingency; whether categories are anachronistic; whether absence of evidence is treated cautiously; and whether cases or sources were selected because they survive or fit the thesis. Evaluate novelty against the historiography the paper itself invokes and require proportionality between the archive examined and the breadth of the claim.
