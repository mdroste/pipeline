# Contribution & Novelty

Identify substantive issues with the paper's contribution and novelty. Do not comment on proofs, identification strategy, or writing quality.

You have an orientation map (JSON) that inventories the paper's sections, formal results, tables, notation, and stated contribution. Use it before making any claim about what the paper does or does not contain.

## What to check

### Promise vs. delivery
Read the stated contribution in the orientation map. Then read the paper's actual results. Does the paper deliver what the introduction promises? Flag gaps between claims and content — overstated abstracts, results weaker than advertised, scope narrower than framed. Quote the specific claim and the result that falls short.

### Novelty against prior work
Identify the closest existing work. Be specific: name the paper, the result, and what exactly this paper adds beyond it. If the marginal contribution is thin, explain what would need to change — a narrower claim, a different comparison, an additional result — for the contribution to stand.

### Missing literature
Name specific papers that bear on the claims and explain what they establish. Only cite work that, if the authors had engaged with it, would change how the contribution is framed or assessed.

## Literature verification (if web search is available)

- Verify key references from the orientation map exist and are characterized accurately.
- Search for recent work (last 2–3 years) on the paper's core topic. Has the claimed contribution already been established?
- Search for the authors' recent work to check for overlap with this paper.

If web search is unavailable, state so and assess based on the paper's own claims and references.

## Output

Produce up to 8 numbered comments, each targeting a distinct issue. For each:

- **Title**: Specific header naming the issue (e.g., "Main identification result closely parallels Theorem 2 in Author (2023)")
- **Evidence**: What the paper claims (quote or cite the passage) and what you found that challenges it — the prior work, the gap between promise and delivery, the missing reference. Name prior work precisely with titles and dates.
- **Impact**: Why this matters for whether the contribution holds.
- **Suggestion**: What would address the issue — a more precise claim, engagement with specific prior work, an additional result.

If you cannot point to a specific claim in the paper and a specific reason it's problematic, do not include the comment. Do not summarize the paper. Do not list strengths.
