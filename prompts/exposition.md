# Exposition & Framing

Identify exposition problems that impede understanding of the main results. Do not comment on correctness or identification.

You have an orientation map (JSON) that inventories the paper's structure, stated contribution, and notation. Use it to understand the organization before critiquing it.

## What to check

### Undefined or inconsistent notation
Flag symbols used before they are defined, defined differently in different places, or departing from field conventions without justification. Quote the specific passage.

### Buried key information
Is the main result clearly stated within the first two pages? If not, where does it first appear and what stands in the way? Is essential information buried in an appendix while secondary material occupies the main text?

### Framing vs. content mismatch
Does the introduction frame the paper as doing X while the body actually does Y? Are terms defined one way but used another? Quote the conflicting passages.

### Self-contained figures and tables
Can a reader understand each figure/table from its title, labels, and notes alone, without reading the surrounding text? Flag specific ones that fail this test and say what's missing.

### Length and proportion
Is substantial space devoted to content that doesn't advance the main results? Name the specific sections and explain what could be cut or moved.

## Output

Produce up to 8 numbered comments targeting the issues that most impede understanding. For each:

- **Title**: Specific header (e.g., "Main theorem not stated until page 14; reader must absorb 12 definitions first")
- **In the paper**: Quote or cite the specific passage, section, or figure where the problem occurs.
- **The problem**: Why it impedes understanding — be concrete.
- **Suggestion**: A specific change (reorder X before Y, add a caption to Figure 3, move Section 4.2 to the appendix).
- **Location**: Page, section, or passage reference.

NOT: "The writing is unclear."
THIS: "Section 3.2 (p. 8) introduces ψ, Γ, Λ in a single paragraph before the reader knows why principal components matter. The motivation in Section 4.1 should precede these definitions."

Every comment must reference a specific location and propose a concrete fix. Do not summarize the paper. Do not list strengths.
