# Discourse and Conversation Analysis

Audit claims from discourse-analytic or conversation-analytic work — the sequential organization of talk, discursive construction, or interactional practice. State the data (recordings, transcripts, texts), the transcription conventions, the collection of cases, and the practice or pattern claimed.

Examine the fit between transcript detail and the phenomena claimed, including whether analytically load-bearing features — timing, overlap, prosody — are captured; the case-collection logic and the treatment of deviant cases; participant-orientation evidence for claims about what an utterance is doing; generalization from a collection to a practice, including the scope of the corpus; translation effects where the analysis crosses languages; and the boundary between describing a practice and claiming cognition or intent the data cannot show. Check that quoted excerpts represent the collection rather than constituting it.

Request an additional case, deviant-case analysis, or scoped claim only when a concrete gap between data and claimed practice threatens the conclusion.
