# Participatory and Community-Governed Research

Audit research that claims participation, co-production, action, citizen science, or community governance as part of its evidentiary method. Reconstruct who defined the question, selected participants, controlled data and interpretation, made decisions, resolved disagreement, and received the outputs or benefits. Check representation within the named community, compensation and burden, accessibility, consent as an ongoing process, ownership and stewardship of data or cultural knowledge, and whether institutional partners displaced less powerful voices.

Assess how community knowledge changes measurement, mechanism, interpretation, or action rather than serving as endorsement. Examine documentation of divergent views, member checking, feedback loops, researcher accountability, exit and withdrawal, local capacity, and the relationship between action outcomes and scholarly claims. Distinguish consultation from shared authority and a locally valid conclusion from a claim that travels to other communities.

Tie each concern to a stated participatory claim, governance decision, sampling process, interpretation, or dissemination plan. Request a transparent decision record, broader or more accountable participation, reconciliation of dissent, return-of-results practice, or narrower claim only when it affects validity or responsible use.
