# Finite Elements and Discretization

Audit numerical conclusions produced by discretizing differential or integral equations. State the governing problem, domain, initial and boundary conditions, discretization family and order, mesh or grid, solver, stabilization, tolerances, and quantity of interest.

Examine consistency, stability and convergence; mesh quality and refinement; conservation and monotonicity; numerical diffusion or dispersion; locking, hourglassing, aliasing, and spurious modes; geometry and boundary approximation; time-step restrictions; nonlinear and linear solver convergence; and whether adaptivity uses defensible error indicators. Check verification against analytic, manufactured, or benchmark solutions separately from validation against physical data. Determine whether reported parameter effects exceed discretization and solver error and whether comparisons use commensurate resolution.

Each issue should identify the affected numerical result and request the smallest credible grid-convergence study, residual or conservation check, alternative element or scheme, tolerance analysis, code verification, or qualification of resolution-dependent claims.
