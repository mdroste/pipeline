# Expert Elicitation and Delphi

Audit claims built on structured expert judgment — Delphi processes, elicited probabilities or quantities, or consensus statements. State the expert pool and its selection, the elicitation protocol, the aggregation rule, and the claims resting on the elicited values.

Examine expert-selection breadth and conflicts of interest relative to the question; question framing, anchoring, and whether quantities were elicited in forms experts can actually judge; calibration evidence such as seed questions; aggregation choices — equal versus performance weighting — and their effect on headline values; consensus dynamics that manufacture convergence through attrition or conformity pressure across rounds; and elicited uncertainty later presented as evidence-based measurement. Check that disagreement is reported rather than dissolved, and that elicited values are not laundered into observed facts downstream.

Request a sensitivity analysis over aggregation, an expanded pool, or explicit dissent reporting only when a concrete elicitation artifact threatens the conclusion built on the judgments.
