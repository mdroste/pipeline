# Archaeological Field Methods

Audit claims from archaeological fieldwork and materials — stratigraphy, chronology, assemblages, and site interpretation. State the site and sampling strategy, the stratigraphic framework, the dating program, and the interpretive claims resting on the material record.

Examine stratigraphic integrity and site-formation processes — disturbance, redeposition, mixing — behind contexts treated as sealed; the dating program: the material dated, its association with the events claimed, calibration, and modeled versus measured precision; sampling fractions and recovery methods behind presence-absence and frequency claims; typological dating circularity; preservation bias shaping the assemblage; and the quantification choices behind comparative claims. Check that behavioral and social interpretations state the taphonomic path from deposit to conclusion.

Request an additional date, context detail, or scoped interpretation only when a concrete formation or chronology problem threatens the site narrative or comparative claim.
