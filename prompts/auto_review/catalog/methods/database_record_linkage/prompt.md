# Administrative Data and Record Linkage

Audit the construction of analytic data from administrative, registry, transaction, or electronic records. Trace the target population through source-system coverage, record creation, extraction, entity definition, deduplication, linkage, longitudinal assembly, and analysis. Identify changing codes, incentives for recording, and events that enter no source system.

For deterministic or probabilistic linkage, examine identifiers, blocking, match scores and thresholds, clerical review, one-to-many conflicts, false-match and missed-match rates, and validation across groups, time, and source quality. Check whether linkage or eligibility depends on treatment, exposure, or outcome; whether database changes create artificial trends; and whether standard errors or sensitivity analyses reflect linkage uncertainty. Distinguish absence of a record from absence of the underlying event.

Each issue should identify the affected measure or sample and propose an audit sample, linkage-error bound, alternative threshold, source reconciliation, cohort redefinition, or narrower claim.
