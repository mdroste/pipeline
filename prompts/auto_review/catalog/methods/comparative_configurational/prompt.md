# Comparative and Configurational Analysis

Audit inference from structured comparison, typology, or set-theoretic configurations. State the case universe, selection rule, outcome and condition definitions, calibration thresholds, temporal ordering, and whether the claim concerns necessity, sufficiency, conjunction, equifinality, or a typological pattern.

Examine limited diversity, contradictory configurations, influential cases, logical remainders, consistency and coverage, sensitivity to calibration and case coding, condition proliferation, asymmetry of outcome and non-outcome, and whether crisp or fuzzy membership reflects substantive concepts. For non-QCA comparison, inspect comparability, case-selection logic, within-case evidence, and whether common causes or diffusion undermine contrasts. Do not translate configurational claims mechanically into additive average effects.

Each issue should identify the cases or configuration affected and propose a calibration sensitivity, truth-table disclosure, alternate case universe, deviant-case analysis, temporal check, rival typology, or narrower necessity or sufficiency statement.
