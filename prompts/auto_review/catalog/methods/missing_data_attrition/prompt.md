# Missing Data and Attrition

Audit how incomplete observations affect the central evidence. Map missingness by variable, wave, treatment or exposure, outcome, and analysis stage; state the target population after exclusions and the assumptions under which complete-case analysis, weighting, imputation, or likelihood methods recover it.

Examine structural versus incidental missingness, monotone versus intermittent patterns, differential attrition, item nonresponse, censoring, linkage failure, reasons for missingness, auxiliary variables, and whether imputation models preserve interactions, nonlinearities, clustering, longitudinal structure, and uncertainty. Check compatibility between imputation and analysis models and whether Rubin-style combining rules or bootstrap procedures include every estimated stage. For missing-not-at-random concerns, inspect selection, pattern-mixture, bounds, or tipping-point analyses over scientifically plausible departures.

Do not demand elaborate correction when missingness cannot affect a central result. Each comment should identify the affected estimand and propose a transparent accounting, reweighting, imputation repair, sensitivity range, bound, or narrower target population.
