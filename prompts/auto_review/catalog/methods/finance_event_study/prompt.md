# Financial Event Studies

Audit claims built on security-price or market reactions around events. State the event set and how it was constructed, announcement versus implementation timing, the expected-return model, the estimation and event windows, and the abnormal-return estimand.

Examine event-date accuracy and information leakage before the window; clustering of events in calendar time and the cross-correlation it induces in inference; the expected-return benchmark's adequacy for the sample's risk profile; confounding contemporaneous news; selection into the event sample conditioned on outcomes; long-horizon drift claims and their sensitivity to benchmark and weighting choices; and whether thin trading or microstructure effects distort measured reactions. Check that economic-magnitude statements aggregate abnormal returns coherently across events and firms.

Require an alternative benchmark, window, or inference scheme only when a concrete bias channel threatens a central reaction estimate. Do not demand robustness to every factor model in the literature.
