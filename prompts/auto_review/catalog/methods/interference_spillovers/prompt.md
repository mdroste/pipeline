# Interference and Spillovers

Audit causal claims where units plausibly affect one another — through markets, networks, geography, or shared resources — so that no-interference assumptions fail. State the claimed estimand under interference (direct, spillover, total, or overall effects), the exposure mapping, and the design or model used to separate them.

Examine whether the randomization or identification level matches the interference structure; contamination of comparison units that inflates or deflates direct-effect estimates; exposure mappings that assume away long-range or general-equilibrium channels; displacement and dilution reinterpreted as treatment effects; cluster definitions that cut across real interaction boundaries; and inference under network or spatial dependence. Check that policy conclusions state which estimand they use — a partial-equilibrium direct effect does not license an at-scale claim.

Require a redesigned comparison, exposure-robust estimator, or bounded claim only when a concrete interference channel threatens a central conclusion. Do not demand interference modeling where isolation is credible.
