# Observational Epidemiology

Audit etiologic, prognostic, or burden claims from population health data. State the source population, sampling or surveillance system, eligibility, exposure and outcome definitions, time zero, follow-up, causal contrast or descriptive target, and population to which results are generalized.

Examine selection into the cohort or control group, prevalent-user and immortal-time biases, exposure timing and latency, outcome ascertainment, diagnostic change, confounding by indication and health-seeking behavior, time-varying confounding, measurement error, loss to follow-up, competing risks, clustering, and transport across calendar periods and populations. For case-control designs, inspect control sampling and whether the odds ratio estimates the claimed quantity. For burden estimates, inspect denominators, underascertainment, standardization, and uncertainty across data sources.

Each issue should connect a population or timing defect to a central estimate and propose a target-trial clarification, alternate risk set, negative control, validation study, weighting or sensitivity analysis, bound, or narrower population claim.
