# Mendelian Randomization

Audit causal claims that use genetic variants as instruments. State the exposure, the outcome, variant selection and its discovery samples, the estimators used, and the population to which the estimate is claimed to apply.

Examine horizontal pleiotropy and whether the robust estimators invoked genuinely address the plausible violation rather than restating it; weak-instrument bias with many variants and winner's curse from discovery-sample overlap; confounding through population stratification, assortative mating, and dynastic effects; the homogeneity or monotonicity assumptions behind point identification; selection bias from survival or study participation; and whether the lifelong exposure window implied by genetic variation matches the clinical or policy interpretation drawn. Check that consistency across estimators is treated as triangulation, not proof.

Request a sensitivity analysis, negative-control outcome, or within-family design only when a concrete violation could reverse or materially shrink the headline effect.
