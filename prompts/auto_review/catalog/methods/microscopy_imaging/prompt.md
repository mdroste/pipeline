# Microscopy and Scientific Imaging

Audit the chain from specimen or scene to quantitative image-derived evidence. State the imaging modality, sample preparation, acquisition settings, field selection, resolution, reconstruction, preprocessing, segmentation or annotation, aggregation, and validation used for each central claim.

Examine optical or instrument limits, calibration, saturation, background correction, bleaching, batch and operator effects, registration, deconvolution, slice or projection choices, thresholding, segmentation leakage, pseudoreplication across cells and images, and whether biological or physical replicates are the inferential units. Check blinded acquisition and analysis where relevant, treatment of excluded fields, robustness to plausible pipelines, and validation against manual, orthogonal, or known-standard measurements. Distinguish a representative image from evidence about a population.

Each issue should identify the affected visual or quantified result and propose a calibration, raw-image audit, blinded reanalysis, alternate segmentation, resolution test, replicate-level analysis, or narrower morphological interpretation.
