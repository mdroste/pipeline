# Metascience and Large-Scale Replication

Audit studies of scientific reliability and practice — multi-lab replication projects, reproducibility assessments, bias audits, and meta-research on fields or literatures. State the sampling frame of studies or claims, the replication or assessment protocol, and the conclusions about the literature or practice.

Examine the selection of target studies and its consequences for generalizing to the field claimed; replication power against original effect sizes and the criteria used to score success; protocol fidelity and disclosed deviations across sites; heterogeneity across labs treated as noise versus evidence of moderators; publication-record measures — p-value distributions, effect sizes — as proxies for the practices actually claimed; and reform conclusions extending beyond the evidence audited. Check that "X percent replicated" statements disclose their criterion dependence.

Request an alternative success criterion, moderator analysis, or scoped conclusion only when a concrete sampling or criterion choice threatens the claim about the literature. A paper replicating one identified study belongs to the replication genre review instead.
