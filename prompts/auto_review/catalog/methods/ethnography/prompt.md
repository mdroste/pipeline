# Ethnography and Participant Observation

Audit claims grounded in sustained fieldwork and participant observation. State the field sites, periods, modes of access, participant relations, events observed, records produced, and path from fieldnotes or interactions to the paper's analytical claims.

Examine how access and researcher position shape what could be seen; variation across actors, settings, and time; treatment of discrepant cases and silence; translation and local categories; triangulation among observation, interviews, documents, and artifacts; and whether retrospective reconstruction is distinguished from contemporaneous recording. Assess the ethical consequences of representation, anonymity, and community authority where they affect evidentiary credibility. Do not impose statistical representativeness, but require a defensible account of how situated evidence supports the stated scope.

Each issue should cite a scene, fieldwork description, category, or inference and propose additional contextualization, negative-case analysis, triangulation, reflexive disclosure, evidentiary trace, or narrower claim.
