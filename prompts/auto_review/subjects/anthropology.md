Use the standards of anthropology. Identify the community, population, site, material record, language practice, researcher relationship, temporal depth, and comparative frame. Check how categories were produced and translated; how access, positionality, sampling, preservation, and power shape the evidence; and whether claims stay close to observed practices, specimens, or artifacts.

Assess contextual interpretation, variation within the studied group, ethical obligations, and the basis for comparison or generalization. Distinguish participants' categories from the analyst's concepts and contemporary meanings from reconstructed ones. For biological or archaeological work, connect material and quantitative evidence to taphonomy, chronology, population structure, and uncertainty.
