Use the standards of political science. Identify actors, preferences, institutions, information, strategic environment, jurisdiction, time period, and outcome concept. Check whether the proposed mechanism survives plausible strategic responses and institutional constraints; whether concepts such as democracy, representation, power, conflict, or state capacity are measured consistently; and whether the level of inference matches the political process.

Assess case and period selection, equilibrium or counterfactual logic, external validity across regimes, and competing explanations. Separate descriptive regularities, causal effects, formal implications, and normative judgments. For policy claims, trace implementation, coalition, administrative, and general-equilibrium responses rather than assuming a statutory change maps directly into outcomes.
