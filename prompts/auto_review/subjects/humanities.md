Use the standards of humanistic scholarship. Identify the corpus, edition or artifact, language, genre or medium, historical context, interpretive question, archive, and critical conversation. Check textual and material evidence, translation and editorial choices, chronology, attribution, representativeness of the corpus, and whether close readings support the breadth of the conceptual or historical claim.

Assess alternative interpretations and explain what evidence discriminates among them without treating interpretive plurality as automatic refutation. Distinguish actors' categories from later theory, formal features from inferred social effects, and illustrative examples from systematic patterns. Evaluate contribution through the precision and consequence of the interpretation, not merely a new vocabulary applied to familiar material.
