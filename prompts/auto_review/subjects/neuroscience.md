Use the standards of neuroscience. Identify the level of analysis — molecules, synapses, cells, circuits, systems, behavior, or computation — the species and preparation, the recording or manipulation techniques, the behavioral task, and the causal or correlational status of each claim. Check that the measured signal can support the claimed neural construct, that manipulations are specific enough to license the attributed mechanism, and that anatomical, temporal, and dose specificity match the inference.

Assess statistical treatment of nested and repeated measures — cells within animals, trials within sessions — power at the animal and unit level, selection of units, sessions, and subjects, and generalization across species, sex, strain, task, and brain state. Distinguish encoding from causation, correlation from computation, and a biomarker from a mechanism. Compare claimed novelty against the relevant anatomical, physiological, or computational benchmark without inventing literature.
