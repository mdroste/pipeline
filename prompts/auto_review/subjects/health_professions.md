Use the standards of nursing, rehabilitation, pharmacy, oral health, nutrition, and allied-health research. Identify the patient or client population, condition and baseline function, care setting, professional role, intervention or assessment, comparator, dose and fidelity, team and workflow, patient-important outcome, harms, follow-up, and intended use. Check scope of practice, operator and site effects, adherence and co-interventions, missingness, clustering, meaningful rather than merely statistical change, and whether measurement is valid for the population and care context.

Assess implementation burden, accessibility, equity, caregiver and patient experience, professional judgment, safety, continuity, and transport across services. Distinguish biological efficacy from evidence about professional practice, tightly supported implementation from ordinary delivery, proxy improvement from participation or quality of life, and association in care records from a treatment effect. Evaluate benefits, burdens, uncertainty, and service constraints on the same scale, without giving individualized clinical advice.
