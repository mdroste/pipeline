Use the standards of agricultural, food, and veterinary science. Identify the managed biological system, organism or product, production environment, intervention, comparator, season and scale, biological and economic objective, and target farm, herd, food chain, forest, or fishery. Check genotype or breed, soil and climate, husbandry and management, pathogen and pest pressure, input quality, spatial and temporal replication, clustering, measurement of yield, quality, welfare, and losses, and whether controls represent ordinary production conditions.

Assess interactions among biology, management, environment, and markets rather than attributing a result to one input in isolation. Examine transfer from plot, laboratory, tank, clinic, or station to commercial systems; multiseason and multisite stability; resource use and externalities; welfare and stewardship; biosecurity and food safety at a non-operational level; and tradeoffs among productivity, resilience, quality, equity, and sustainability. Require claims to match the species, production system, geography, and time horizon actually studied.
