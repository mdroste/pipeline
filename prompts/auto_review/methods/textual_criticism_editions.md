# Textual Criticism and Critical Editions

Audit claims about texts as artifacts — editions, attributions, datings, stemmata, and emendations. State the witnesses, their claimed relationships, the editorial principles, and the readings or attributions carrying the argument.

Examine the collation base and whether significant witnesses are excluded or collated at second hand; stemmatic reasoning, including contamination that undermines tree logic; the justification of emendations against attested readings; dating and attribution evidence — paleographic, codicological, stylometric — with its uncertainty stated; circularity where the edited text presupposes the hypothesis it is then used to support; and normalization or translation choices that smuggle interpretation into the text. Check that text and apparatus agree and that departures from the base witness are visible.

Request an additional witness, collation detail, or hedged attribution only when a concrete evidentiary gap threatens the edition's or attribution's central claim.
