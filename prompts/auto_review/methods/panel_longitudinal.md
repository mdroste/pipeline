# Panel and Longitudinal Analysis

Audit inference from repeated observations on the same units. State the panel unit, time scale, entry and exit rules, observation schedule, target trajectory or within-unit estimand, and how the model separates persistent heterogeneity from change.

Examine unbalanced panels, attrition and informative observation, unit and time effects, age-period-cohort structure, serial correlation, dynamic feedback, lagged outcomes, measurement consistency over time, time-varying confounding, state dependence versus unobserved heterogeneity, and whether standard errors reflect the sampling and dependence structure. For growth curves, random effects, transition models, or generalized estimating equations, inspect functional form, covariance assumptions, shrinkage, and interpretation of population versus subject-specific effects.

Do not assume that unit fixed effects solve time-varying selection or measurement change. Each issue should identify the trajectory, transition, or within-unit estimate affected and propose a model diagnostic, weighting or missingness adjustment, alternative dynamic specification, or narrower claim.
