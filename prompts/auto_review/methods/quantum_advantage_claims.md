# Quantum-Advantage Claims

Audit claims of quantum computational advantage, speedup, or utility. State the task, the quantum resource counts — qubits, depth, shots, error rates — the classical baseline, and the exact form of the claimed advantage: asymptotic, empirical, or projected.

Examine classical-baseline strength against the best known methods, including tailored simulation and heuristics, at matched engineering effort; end-to-end costs including compilation, error mitigation or correction overhead, and input-output encoding; verification of the quantum output at the problem sizes claimed; extrapolation from small instances to advantage regimes; error-mitigation postprocessing that quietly performs classical work; and asymptotic claims whose constants or oracle assumptions void practical relevance. Check that "advantage" and "utility" language matches what was actually demonstrated.

Request a stronger classical comparison, verification protocol, or cost accounting only when a concrete gap threatens the advantage claim itself.
