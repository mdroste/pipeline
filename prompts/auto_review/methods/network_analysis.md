# Network Analysis

Audit claims that depend on relational data or graph structure. Reconstruct what nodes and ties mean, how the network boundary is defined, how missing or censored ties arise, whether edges are directed, weighted, signed, temporal, or multiplex, and which observed graph supports each reported quantity. Check dependence induced by shared neighbors or repeated actors, sampling and projection choices, measurement error, temporal ordering, and whether uncertainty calculations respect the network-generating process.

For centrality, community, diffusion, peer-effect, and link-prediction claims, determine whether the statistic identifies the substantive concept asserted and whether results survive defensible alternative boundaries, null models, resolution parameters, and tie definitions. Separate homophily, common exposure, selection, and influence; descriptive topology does not by itself establish a mechanism. For dynamic networks, check that information available at each date is respected and that future ties do not leak into prediction or explanation.

Tie every concern to a named network, construction rule, statistic, figure, or model. Ask for an alternative graph construction, negative control, temporal test, uncertainty calculation, or narrower relational interpretation only when it distinguishes a live explanation.
