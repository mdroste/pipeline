# Survey Experiments and Conjoint Designs

Audit causal claims from experiments embedded in surveys — vignettes, framing and list experiments, and conjoint designs. State the manipulated attributes, the estimand (such as average marginal component effects or framing contrasts), the sample and its recruitment, and the outcome measures.

Examine attention and satisficing and their interaction with treatment; profile realism and attribute-collinearity restrictions in conjoints; the reference-category dependence of component-effect interpretations, and whether marginal means are needed for subgroup comparisons; masking logic in list and endorsement designs, including design effects and floor-ceiling failures; carryover and ordering across repeated tasks; and whether hypothetical stated choices support the behavioral or policy conclusion drawn. Check that subgroup contrasts respect the randomization.

Require a manipulation check, alternative estimand, or external behavioral benchmark only when a concrete gap between the survey response and the claimed construct threatens the conclusion.
