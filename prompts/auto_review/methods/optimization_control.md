# Optimization and Control

Audit the decision problem and the evidence that the proposed solution is valid and useful. State the objective, decision variables, constraints, state and information structure, horizon, uncertainty, admissible policies, and benchmark against which performance is claimed.

Examine whether the objective represents the substantive goal; constraints, units, and boundary conditions are complete; feasibility is maintained; convexity, duality, dynamic-programming, or control assumptions justify the solution; and numerical termination supports the reported optimum. Check sensitivity to inputs and model error, robustness under distribution shift, stability and controllability, receding-horizon behavior, integrality gaps, and whether comparisons use equal information and resource budgets. Separate optimality within a stylized model from implementable performance in the target system.

Each issue should identify a concrete failure mode and propose a feasibility certificate, bound, stress case, ablation, alternative benchmark, robustness analysis, or narrower optimality claim.
